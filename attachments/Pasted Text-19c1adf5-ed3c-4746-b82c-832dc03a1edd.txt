Absolutely. Here is a build-ready blueprint for HandyPix AI—from the homeowner taking the first photo through AI diagnosis, bidding, scheduling, payment, completion, and reviews.

HandyPix AI

Tagline: Snap it. Diagnose it. Fix it.

Core value proposition

> Take a picture of what's wrong. HandyPix AI helps identify the likely issue, gets bids from vetted local professionals, lets you compare them, and handles the job and payment in one place.



One important product principle: AI should provide a preliminary diagnosis, not present itself as a guaranteed professional inspection. For electrical, gas, structural, roofing, plumbing, HVAC, or other potentially dangerous situations, the app should clearly recommend professional inspection and appropriate emergency services when warranted.

37. AI should not just identify the object

The AI needs to answer structured questions.

For example:

{
  "category": "plumbing",
  "problem": "possible drain connection leak",
  "visual_evidence": [
    "water accumulation",
    "moisture near connection"
  ],
  "possible_causes": [
    "worn gasket",
    "loose connection"
  ],
  "risk_level": "normal",
  "professional_inspection": false,
  "additional_questions": [
    "Does it leak only when the faucet runs?"
  ]
}

That structured output can then feed your job creation system.

38. AI confidence architecture

Don't simply tell users:

> "Your pipe is broken."



Instead:

> Possible issue: loose drain connection
AI confidence: Moderate
Verification: A handyman should inspect the connection before repair.



For high-risk categories:

> The photo isn't sufficient to safely determine the cause. A qualified professional should inspect this issue.



This distinction will be important for both user trust and liability management.

# HandyPix AI — Build Blueprint

HandyPix AI should launch as a **photo-to-repair marketplace**: homeowners photograph a problem, receive a safety-aware AI assessment, post a structured job, compare local professional bids, book, pay, and retain a verified home-repair history. The strongest logo direction from the supplied concepts is the **camera/aperture mark**: it immediately communicates both visual diagnosis and repair services. [1][2][3]

**Tagline:** *Snap it. Diagnose it. Fix it.*

> **Critical product rule:** AI provides a preliminary visual assessment, never a guaranteed inspection, repair instruction, or emergency determination.

| Handyman / Pro | Sets service area and skills, views matched jobs, submits bids, performs work, receives payouts |
| Admin / Operations | Verifies pros, monitors jobs and payments, handles disputes, reviews AI safety flags |
| Support agent | Handles account, booking, payment, cancellation, and escalation tickets |

For launch, use “Pro” in the consumer-facing UI rather than “handyman” everywhere. It supports a broader provider base: electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, carpenters, painters, appliance repair specialists, and general repair professionals.

## Safety layer

Every diagnosis should receive a risk classification before the customer can post the job.

| Level | UI behavior | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Green: Routine | Continue to job creation | Loose handle, small drywall hole, dripping faucet |
| Yellow: Pro recommended | Require acknowledgment before posting | HVAC issue, roof damage, recurring leak, electrical outlet issue |
| Red: Potentially dangerous | Prioritize emergency guidance and limit ordinary bidding flow | Exposed wiring, possible gas leak, active fire, major flooding, collapse risk |

### Red-risk screen

```text
Potential safety risk detected

The photos may indicate an issue that could be dangerous.
Do not attempt a repair yourself.

[ Call Emergency Services ]
[ Find Emergency Professional ]
[ I Am Safe — View Guidance ]
```

**Rules**
- Possible gas leak: advise leaving the area, avoiding switches/flames, and contacting the utility/emergency authority.
- Fire or smoke: direct to emergency services.
- Electrical exposure with water: tell the customer not to touch the area or attempt repairs.
- Flooding near electrical sources: issue a prominent safety warning.
- Structural instability: tell users to leave unsafe areas and seek qualified assessment.

Have a legal professional review all emergency language for each launch market.

## AI diagnosis flow

### Vision-to-job pipeline

```text
1. Customer uploads photo/video
             |
             v
2. Media validation
   - File type and size
   - Malware scan
   - Blur/quality detection
   - Duplicate detection
   - EXIF privacy handling
             |
             v
3. Vision analysis
   - Identify probable category
   - Extract visible evidence
   - Identify likely issue candidates
             |
             v
4. Safety classifier
   - Routine
   - Professional inspection recommended
   - Potentially dangerous
             |
             v
5. Follow-up question engine
   - Ask only high-value questions
   - Collect symptoms and context
             |
             v
6. Structured diagnosis output
             |
             v
7. AI job-description generator
             |
             v
8. Customer review/edit/confirmation
             |
             v
9. Matching and job publication
```

### Guardrails

- Never let raw model text directly control payment, emergency messaging, job status, or account enforcement.
- Validate all AI responses against a strict JSON schema.
- Maintain category-specific safety rules outside the LLM.
- Use deterministic rules for clearly dangerous keywords and visual categories.
- Log model output and final user-facing output separately.
- Permit users to override the suggested category.
- Add human review queues for high-risk, low-confidence, or repeated safety cases.
- Conduct regular evaluation using labeled repair images and expert-reviewed scenarios.

### Example schema

```ts
type Diagnosis = {
  category: "plumbing" | "electrical" | "hvac" | "roofing" | "carpentry" | "general_repair";
  possibleIssue: string;
  confidence: "low" | "moderate" | "high";
  riskLevel: "normal" | "professional_review" | "potentially_dangerous";
  visualEvidence: string[];
  possibleCauses: string[];
  followUpQuestions: string[];
  customerSafeGuidance: string[];
  professionalInspectionRecommended: boolean;
  jobSummary: string;
};
```

## Matching system

### Pro matching filters

A pro should receive a job only if they satisfy hard eligibility requirements:

- Approved and active account
- Category/skill match
- Service area match
- Availability overlap
- Payment onboarding completed
- Required insurance/licensing status, where applicable
- Not blocked by the customer
- Not suspended or under serious investigation

### Ranking factors

Use ranking only after applying hard filters.

```text
match_score =
  distance_score
+ skill_relevance_score
+ availability_score
+ response_rate_score
+ completion_rate_score
+ rating_quality_score
+ repeat_customer_affinity
- cancellation_penalty
- unresolved_complaint_penalty
```

Do not display a mysterious “AI score” to customers. Instead show concrete explanations:

- “Available Saturday morning”
- “Plumbing specialist”
- “4.9 stars from 127 completed jobs”
- “3.1 miles away”
- “Usually responds within 15 minutes”

For the MVP, send a job to a limited initial batch, such as 10–25 qualifying pros, then expand outreach if bids do not arrive.

## Trust and security

### Essential protections

- Encrypt data in transit and at rest.
- Use private media storage and short-lived signed URLs.
- Strip unnecessary EXIF metadata from user-uploaded images.
- Enforce authorization checks at the record level, not only in UI.
- Use rate limiting and bot defenses on auth, upload, bidding, and payment endpoints.
- Use idempotency keys for booking, payment, and payout endpoints.
- Verify payment webhooks cryptographically.
- Maintain immutable audit records for money and status changes.
- Run malware scanning on uploads.
- Add moderation/reporting flows for messages, reviews, and images.
- Use secure secrets management rather than `.env` files in production.
- Implement account-deletion and data-retention workflows.
- Obtain explicit consent for location sharing and notifications.

### Legal and operational review

Before release, engage legal and compliance counsel for:
- Marketplace terms of service
- Contractor classification
- Licensing requirements by service and state
- Insurance and verification claims
- Payment flow and fund handling
- Privacy policy and biometric/location data handling
- AI disclosures and liability language
- Dispute/refund policy
- Consumer protection and cancellation rules

## Success criteria

The MVP is working when customers can reliably complete this journey without support:

```text
Open app
-> Upload photo
-> Receive appropriate AI assessment
-> Publish job
-> Get bids
-> Select a pro
-> Pay and book
-> Track the repair
-> Confirm completion
-> Review
```

The business is working when the marketplace achieves sufficient local liquidity: customers receive several relevant bids quickly, good pros win enough work to return, payment/dispute operations are manageable, and AI improves job quality without overstating its certainty.
